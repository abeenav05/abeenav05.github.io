Thunderbikes Electric Cover

A carbon fibre electric cover for UBC Thunderbikes' MotoStudent electric motorcycle. I lead the sub-project, from surface modelling through structural validation to composite prototypes.

0.005 mm max displacement vs. 10 mm competition limit

Read the details
  • Role: Aerodynamics subteam member; leading the electric cover sub-project and a team of two since Sept. 2026.
  • Design: Surface-modelled the cover in SolidWorks to be aerodynamically efficient within the bike's packaging constraints; now on version 6.
  • Validation: SolidWorks static structural FEA (6-ply carbon fibre, 50 N pressure load) gave a max displacement of 0.005011 mm. Refining the profile with Ansys CFD to reduce drag (in progress).
  • Manufacturing: Modelled 3D-printed molds and prototyped 3 composite parts by vacuum infusion and wet layup to prove the design can be built.
  • Process: Built a Notion workflow linking every CAD version, mold, FEA run and work log; presented at a design review in March 2026.

Electric Go-Kart

A working electric go-kart built from scrap metal and two repurposed hoverboard hub motors. The only parts I bought were the speed controllers.

20 km/h top speed · under $50

Read the details
  • Frame: Taught myself stick welding (SMAW) and welded a custom frame from an old dolly and recycled metal.
  • Steering: Designed and built a working mechanical steering system out of scrap metal.
  • Electronics: Built the control circuit and programmed an Arduino Uno to map throttle input to PWM outputs for the motor speed controllers.
  • Result: Reached 20 km/h and was driven and demoed in my school's hallway.

Carbon Fibre Skateboard

A skateboard deck with a resawn maple veneer core and carbon fibre skins, built for a lighter, stiffer deck and to take my composites work further outside the team.

Build log coming soon

Read the plan

  • Layup: Laminate the maple core and bond carbon fibre skins using wet layup and vacuum bagging.
